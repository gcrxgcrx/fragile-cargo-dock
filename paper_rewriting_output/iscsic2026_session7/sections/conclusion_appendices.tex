\section{Conclusion}
CREATE turns failed policy training into input for the next reward-design decision. By separating training and evaluation rewards, preserving revision history, and retaining the best reward-policy pair, it implements an autonomous supervisory loop for learning-based control. Across ten reward lineages in two benchmarks, every initially sub-threshold reward is improved beyond the configured task threshold; representative traces show both successful incremental repair and harmful later revisions that are contained by the archive. The results support training-feedback-guided reward evolution as a practical complement to one-shot and population-based reward generation.

\section{Reproducibility and Audit Trail}
The implementation stores a complete record for every reward evaluation. The reward source, policy-training configuration, policy checkpoint, native-evaluation result, component summary, LLM input, LLM response, and revision decision are written beneath the corresponding seed and iteration. Consequently, a reported score can be traced to the exact reward program and PPO configuration that produced it. The paper tables were generated from these archived run artifacts rather than copied from narrative experiment notes.

The task interface given to the language agent contains the observation and action schemas, a natural-language objective, and masked transition-source information needed to interpret the state. Fields that would leak the official reward, a success label, a failure label, or the evaluation score are explicitly forbidden in generated reward code. During policy training the wrapper replaces the native reward before the transition reaches PPO. During evaluation a separate unwrapped environment is created, and the generated reward is not used in $J_t$. This implementation-level separation is stronger than simply instructing the LLM not to copy the benchmark reward.

Table~\ref{tab:audit} summarizes the artifacts needed to reproduce and inspect one evolution step. The active reward lineage remains sequential because $R_{t+1}$ depends on the evidence produced by $R_t$; however, the five search seeds are independent and can be executed in parallel. The archive records both the active final reward and the best reward, which is necessary for correctly interpreting runs in which the last revision regresses.
